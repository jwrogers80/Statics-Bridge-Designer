    Why the cross members read zero. The two plane trusses are identical and the load is split evenly between them, so nothing pushes one plane toward the other and the horizontal cross members carry no axial force. They still earn their mass: they hold the two trusses at the right spacing and brace the compression chords sideways, which is what stops a real bridge from folding over. Because those cross members are horizontal and sit only on the outline, this frame has no diagonal bracing between the planes — analyzed as pure pin-jointed bars in three dimensions it is a mechanism sideways, so the app solves each plane in its own plane and does not check out-of-plane stability. An off-center or angled load would find that weakness; a real entry usually adds diagonals in the top and bottom horizontal planes.

    Self-weight is neglected — the applied load dominates. Joints are assumed as strong as the members, so failure is predicted in the sticks, not the glue. Balsa is a natural material: real strength and stiffness vary by a factor of two or more between sticks, so treat the safety factor as a design comparison, not a guarantee.
